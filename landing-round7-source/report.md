# PodSubs Landing Page, Round 7: The LinkedIn Model

Karl's order (2026-11-17): rebuild the PodSubs landing page following LinkedIn's landing page structure and design method as closely as a PodSubs page can, studied on Mobbin and on the live site. One page only, a design demo for Karl's approval, responsive on desktop and mobile. Everything PodSubs does must be visible on this page, and the page stays open to browse: a visitor can search and open shows, people, guests, and jobs without joining. Joining is asked only when a visitor wants to interact.

Source model: Mobbin's full-page capture of LinkedIn's signed-out landing page (LinkedIn Web, desktop 1512x945). The live signed-out page could not be opened in the research browser because every available window is signed in to LinkedIn, so the Mobbin capture is the structural source, cross-checked against Karl's own reading of the live page. Section order, background rhythm, type treatment, pill mechanics, and footer structure below follow that capture.

## The LinkedIn model, section by section

1. Header, white: wordmark left; right side nav with small icons (Discover, People, Learning, Jobs), a thin divider, Join now as plain text, Sign in as an outlined pill. No search box in the header.
2. Hero, white: very large, light-weight headline in a warm rust-brown tone, left aligned over two lines ("Welcome to your professional community"), a form under it, and a flat illustration on the right.
3. Explore topics, warm cream background (#f3f2ef family): large thin heading left, small-caps grey label right ("CONTENT TOPICS"), pill row with the first pill active (blue text, blue outline, a link to the topics directory), then plain pills.
4. Find the right job, white: same two-column pattern, label "SUGGESTED SEARCHES", job pills, and a "Show more" control that expands the pill list in place.
5. Post your job band, warm cream: short full-width band, heading left, outlined pill button right.
6. Open to work, warm cream continuing with no white break: large thin headline in the same warm rust tone as the hero, one supporting paragraph, and a large circular photo treatment on the right (dark circular backdrop, the photo element inside it).
7. Two columns, white: each column has a flat illustration on top (figure against a large semicircle backdrop, plants), a heading under it, and an outlined pill button.
8. Join band, pale blue gradient sky, full bleed: large thin heading, solid pill button, and a wide flat panorama illustration of workplaces below.
9. Footer, warm cream: link columns (General, Browse, Business Solutions, Directories), then a small-print bar with the logo, copyright, and policy links.

Background rhythm in order: white, cream, white, cream (band and open-to-work as one continuous cream block), white, pale gradient, cream footer.

## Behaviour and honesty rules
- The page is a design demo. Sample search results and any figures are sample data and labelled as such in small print at the footer of the demo.
- The search dropdown, the Show more pill expansion, and the mobile layout must all work in the demo.
- Desktop and mobile are both first-class: the first view fits one screen on a 1440x900 desktop and a 390x844 phone, sections stack cleanly, no sideways scroll at either width.
- Photos are shown whole, never cropped, except the open-to-guest photo which is presented in a circle by design, as LinkedIn presents its open-to-work photo.
- No gradients except the join band's pale sky. No em dashes in copy.

## Round 7 fixes (Karl approved the page 2026-11-17 with these changes)

Karl's verdict: the page is right and he loves it. Three fixes before it goes into the prototype.

1. DESKTOP FIRST VIEW LAYOUT. On desktop the stacked first view leaves one side of the screen empty and looks odd. Rebuild the desktop first view as two columns, the way LinkedIn's hero works and the way the PodSubs first view worked before: the text column on the left (the Welcome to your professional community headline, the styled Welcome to PodSubs answer line, the supporting line, Join the network and Claim your show, and the search pill) and the hero photo on the right of the text column, shown whole on its white mat, vertically centred against the text. The whole first view stays balanced and centred in the page width. MOBILE DOES NOT CHANGE: the stacked order Karl approved stays exactly as it is on phone widths.

2. THE LINE UNDER THE SEARCH. Karl found "Browse everything free. Join only when you want to interact." odd and asked for something else. Replace it with exactly this line: "One search across every show, every person, and every guest in podcasting." Nothing else changes about the search: it still works, with the grouped dropdown of sample results.

3. REAL DESTINATION PAGES. Clicking Shows, People, Guests, or Jobs in the header, and every browse door on the page (the Explore pills, the job pills, Browse guests, Find podcasters you know, and the footer browse links), must take the visitor to an actual page for that hub inside this demo, not a dropdown panel. Build four simple hub pages in the same design system: a Shows page, a People page, a Guests page, and a Jobs page, each with its own heading, its own search field, and a list of sample entries with photos, names, and one line each, all labelled as sample data in the page footer as before. A visitor can open an entry on a hub page and read it without joining; actions that interact (Follow, Apply, Book, Claim, Message) open the join panel, as they do now. The landing page search dropdown stays as a quick aid, but no nav click may end in a panel instead of a page. Every control on the landing page that names a destination must land on that destination.
